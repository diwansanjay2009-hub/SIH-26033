## Problem Title
SIH 26033: Multiple intermediaries reduce farmers earnings and increase consumer prices.

## Problem Creater's Organization
Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution

## Theme
Agriculture, FoodTech & Rural Development

## Problem Description
Expected Solution: Create a digital marketplace that:

• Connects farmers/FPOs directly with consumers and bulk buyers.
• Provides logistics support.
• Uses AI for demand forecasting and route optimization.

Benefits:

• Better prices for farmers.
• Lower prices for consumers.
• Reduced supply chain inefficiencies.

## Proposed Solution

• Direct Digital Marketplace: We propose a comprehensive B2B/B2C digital platform that allows individual farmers and Farmer Producer Organizations (FPOs) to list their harvests online, establishing direct transaction channels with urban retail consumers, restaurant chains, and bulk commercial buyers.
• Elimination of Intermediaries: By bypassing multiple layers of traditional middlemen (commission agents and distributors), the platform ensures transparent price discovery, returning up to 20-30% more value directly to the farmers while keeping prices affordable for final consumers.
• Integrated Value-Added Services: The ecosystem provides built-in digital escrow payments, instant KYC verification for buyers, and localized multi-language support (audio and text) to empower low-literacy rural producers.
## Technical Approach
• Core Stack & Architecture: A cloud-based microservices architecture utilizing Node.js/Python for the backend, React Native for a cross-platform mobile application, and a secure relational database (PostgreSQL) paired with MongoDB for dynamic product listing catalogs.
• AI Demand Forecasting & Dynamic Logistics: We integrate an AI/ML module (built using Python libraries like Scikit-learn and TensorFlow) that analyzes historical market data, seasonal crop cycles, and consumption trends to predict regional demand. This pairs with a dynamic route optimization engine (using genetic algorithms and geographic mapping) to aggregate supply, coordinate rural freight pickups, and calculate fuel-efficient multi-stop delivery routes.
• Operational Flow:
	1. Producer Stage: Farmer logs yield →
	2. Processing Stage: Platform groups local batches via nearby FPOs →
	3. Logistics Stage: AI optimizes pickup trucks to aggregate produce →
	4. Consumer Stage: Cold-chain logistics delivers freshly picked goods directly to hubs or end buyers.
## Feasibility and Viability
• Feasibility Analysis: The solution relies on accessible, existing smartphones and lightweight progressive web app architectures designed to operate smoothly on low-bandwidth rural mobile networks. FPOs serve as natural offline aggregation hubs, making physical drop-offs practical and highly manageable.
• Risks & Adoption Barriers: Key challenges include initial digital skepticism among traditional farmers, lack of smartphone literacy, and the high upfront cost of organizing small-scale transport infrastructure.
• Mitigation Strategies: We mitigate these roadblocks by embedding voice-assisted, vernacular commands directly into the mobile app, providing digital tool training camps through established FPO networks, and partnering with local third-party logistics firms to eliminate the need to buy an expensive fleet of vehicles.

## Impact and Benefits
• Target Audience Impact: Directly supports small and marginal agricultural producers by opening new commercial markets beyond local mandis, while offering urban households and wholesale buyers access to fresh, trackable food at fair market value.
• Socio-Economic Benefits: Maximizes rural household income, reduces food waste at the farmhouse gate, and stabilizes daily consumer prices in dense urban sectors.
• Environmental Gains: Optimizing collection routes lowers greenhouse emissions per ton of food moved, and direct-to-consumer delivery minimizes plastic packaging and organic landfill waste generated by traditional multi-tiered markets.
